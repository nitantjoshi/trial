DCR (Dacryocystorhinostomy)

Dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) is a specialized surgical procedure used to treat a blocked tear duct. When the tear drainage system is blocked, tears cannot drain properly, causing excessive watering, recurrent infections, and swelling near the inner corner of the eye. DCR creates a new drainage pathway between the tear sac and the nasal cavity, restoring normal tear flow.

When is DCR Recommended?

Persistent watery eyes (Epiphora)

Blocked tear duct

Repeated tear sac infections (Dacryocystitis)

Swelling or pain near the inner corner of the eye

Discharge from the tear duct

Types of DCR Surgery

External DCR

Performed through a small incision beside the nose.

High success rate for tear duct blockage.

Suitable for most patients.

Endoscopic (Scarless) DCR

Performed through the nose using an endoscope.

No visible external scar.

Less tissue disruption and faster recovery.

Benefits of DCR

Relieves excessive tearing

Prevents recurrent infections

Restores normal tear drainage

Improves comfort and vision quality

High long-term success rate

Recovery

DCR is usually performed as a day-care procedure. Most patients return home the same day and resume normal activities within a few days. Your ophthalmologist will provide post-operative medications and follow-up care for a smooth recovery.
